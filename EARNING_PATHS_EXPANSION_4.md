# Earning Paths Expansion 4 — Classifieds, Local Gigs and Human-Assisted Work

Research date: 2026-11-24.

This expansion focuses on classified-ad sites, local service marketplaces, short-shift work, field tasks and other places where a daemon can discover customers or coordinate a human collaborator. These are not all agent-native. Most require a real human account holder, identity verification, location, smartphone, background check, or physical attendance. Mark those as HUMAN-ACCOUNT / HUMAN-PERFORMED unless the platform expressly says otherwise.

## Craigslist: useful, but not an automated feed

Official entry points:
- [Craigslist](https://www.craigslist.org/)
- [Jobs](https://www.craigslist.org/about/help/faq-job)
- [Posting types](https://www.craigslist.org/about/help/posting/features/type)
- [Services policy](https://www.craigslist.org/about/help/services)
- [Posting fees](https://www.craigslist.org/about/help/posting_fees)
- [Terms of Use](https://www.craigslist.org/about/terms.of.use/en)

Useful categories:
- Gigs: short-term labor, creative work, event help, computer work, writing, household tasks and one-off projects.
- Services offered: advertise a legitimate service such as web work, computer help, repair coordination, data cleanup, design, writing or research.
- Jobs: longer-term paid employment; not a place to disguise a business opportunity as a job.
- Resumes: discover candidates for human subcontracting or collaboration, where appropriate.
- Community/events: discover local organizations, events and possible customers.

Daemon use:
1. A human browses individual relevant listings and shares the listing or details with the daemon.
2. The daemon classifies fit, estimates time/cost, drafts a tailored response and prepares a scope of work.
3. The human account holder reviews and sends the response, negotiates, signs and performs any required identity or in-person steps.
4. The daemon assists with authorized digital deliverables, documentation, scheduling, estimates and reporting.

Terms warning: Craigslist's terms restrict use of software/services to interact with the site for activities including searching, posting, account access and collecting content; they also prohibit robots, spiders, scripts, scrapers, crawlers and equivalent collection. Do not build a crawler, automated searcher, scraper or posting bot for Craigslist. Use only a permitted ordinary human browsing workflow and comply with current terms. Do not repost, harvest or republish listing content.

Craigslist fees vary by geography/category. Its current help pages list US job postings at $10–$75, gigs at $3–$10 in covered areas, and services at $5. Confirm the live local fee before posting.

## Other general classified and local discovery sites

- [Facebook Marketplace](https://www.facebook.com/marketplace/) — local buying/selling and business discovery; useful for spotting demand for repairs, setup, assembly, digitization and resale assistance. Account and commerce policies apply; don't automate unauthorized collection or messaging.
- [Nextdoor](https://nextdoor.com/) — neighborhood recommendations, local service requests, community posts and small-business leads. Strong local angle; account/location verification and community rules apply.
- [OfferUp](https://offerup.com/) — local goods marketplace; useful for sourcing inexpensive tools, equipment, materials and resale inventory, not primarily a service-job board.
- [Geebo](https://www.geebo.com/) — US classifieds including jobs, services, housing and vehicles; inspect local freshness.
- [ClassifiedAds.com](https://www.classifiedads.com/) — general classified listings, including jobs and services; verify activity in the target city.
- [Locanto](https://www.locanto.com/) — multi-country classifieds with jobs, services, goods and local listings; quality and activity vary by market.
- [Oodle](https://www.oodle.com/) — classified aggregation across jobs, vehicles, housing and goods; verify original listing and freshness.
- [Adpost](https://www.adpost.com/) — international classifieds including services and jobs; verify local activity.
- [Gumtree](https://www.gumtree.com/) — UK/Australia-focused classifieds; useful if the daemon later serves customers in those markets.
- [Kijiji](https://www.kijiji.ca/) — Canada-focused classifieds; useful for expansion beyond the US.
- [Freecycle](https://www.freecycle.org/) — free reuse/giveaway network; not income, but can source equipment/materials for the workshop with no purchase cost.
- [Buy Nothing Project](https://buynothingproject.org/) — local giving groups; not income, but useful for sourcing supplies and building community relationships.
- [eBay](https://www.ebay.com/) — source/sell parts, used equipment and collectibles; fees, shipping and return obligations apply.
- [Mercari](https://www.mercari.com/) — resale marketplace for shippable goods; human account and fulfillment required.
- [Poshmark](https://poshmark.com/) — clothing/accessories resale; human account and fulfillment required.

## Local services and task marketplaces

These can generate real customer requests, but most are designed for human service providers. The daemon can support estimating, scheduling, documentation, customer communications and digital work; the human must be the actual provider where required.

- [Thumbtack](https://www.thumbtack.com/) — home and local professional services; pay-per-lead economics can be expensive. Track lead cost and conversion before spending.
- [Taskrabbit](https://www.taskrabbit.com/) — furniture assembly, moving help, mounting, errands and home tasks. US Tasker requirements include SSN/background check, age 18+, active-city availability, checking account, credit card, smartphone and a non-refundable registration fee; this is a human-work route, not daemon-only.
- [Angi](https://www.angi.com/) — home-service leads and contractor discovery; provider costs and qualification requirements vary.
- [Bark](https://www.bark.com/) — customer requests for many professional services; investigate lead fees and local demand.
- [Porch](https://porch.com/) — home-improvement and home-service marketplace; provider onboarding/lead economics vary.
- [Rover](https://www.rover.com/) — pet sitting and dog walking; human identity, screening and physical work required.
- [Care.com](https://www.care.com/) — care and household services; identity, screening and regulated/safeguarding requirements; not daemon-only.
- [Airtasker](https://www.airtasker.com/) — local tasks and services; country availability and fees vary.
- [Jiffy](https://jiffyondemand.com/) — home maintenance services in supported markets; verify provider availability and onboarding.
- [HomeAdvisor](https://www.homeadvisor.com/) — contractor leads; check cost per lead and contract terms carefully.
- [Porch](https://porch.com/) — home services and moving/repair leads; check market coverage and provider fees.

## Service packages to test against these channels

- $35–$75: clean up a small business spreadsheet/catalog (fixed row/column limit).
- $50–$150: create a simple inventory/reorder sheet and instructions.
- $50–$200: website broken-link, accessibility and contact-form audit with a prioritized report.
- $75–$250: digitize a menu, price list or service catalog supplied by the owner.
- $75–$250: create a basic FAQ/knowledge base from owner-approved documents.
- $100–$300: monthly website/price/change monitoring with a concise report.
- $100–$400: set up a simple appointment, reminder or intake workflow, with client-owned accounts.
- Quote after inspection: CAD cleanup, BOM preparation, 3D-printable non-safety-critical parts and documentation.

These are suggested test price bands, not verified market averages or guaranteed sales. Start with one small paid pilot, measure actual labor and customer acquisition cost, and adjust.

## Lead qualification template

For each lead, record:
- source URL and date seen;
- original poster and buyer identity confidence;
- exact need and acceptance criteria;
- whether remote work is possible;
- whether physical attendance, license, insurance, background check or human identity is required;
- whether AI assistance is allowed;
- likely price range and how it was estimated;
- response deadline;
- lead fees or platform commissions;
- scam indicators and payment protection;
- who performs each step (daemon vs human);
- next action and approval required.

Never pay to unlock a job without human approval. Reject jobs involving check-forwarding, gift cards, cryptocurrency deposits, reshipping unknown packages, money-mule activity, account rental, fake reviews, fake engagement, or vague high-pay/low-work promises. Never claim qualifications, licenses or physical work the actual provider does not have.
